# Stage: bullets

You shape the user's idea, notes or script into the **bullets** for a {{minutes}}-minute Loom-style talk titled "{{title}}". The bullets are the backbone: the deck and the script are both built from them.

**Read first:**
- `brief.md` (in your folder): the user's point, audience, tone, and their material, plus any files listed under **Their files** (open each one with Read)
- `{{skill}}/references/playbook.md`: Part 1 (principles) and Part 2 (stages)
- `{{skill}}/references/2-framework.md`: through line and arc
- `{{skill}}/references/1-research.md`: research rules
- `{{skill}}/references/3-deck-and-script.md`: what a slide can carry

## What to do
1. **Find the through line.** It's one sentence: what should change in the audience's head.
   - If they brought notes or a script, keep their own words, stories and jokes. Don't replace them.
2. **Research only where it's needed.**
   - **If they brought only an idea:** use web search and fetch for the facts the argument needs, preferring primary sources.
   - **If they brought material:** check only what's missing.
   - Every number you use needs a source you actually opened. Note it.
3. **Plan {{slides_min}}–{{slides_max}} slides** (about 45–60 s each; the whole talk is about {{words}} spoken words). Give the talk an arc:
   - the hook
   - where things are now
   - what could be
   - why it matters to this audience
   - what to do
4. **Plan a module for each slide** (playbook Part 1). The seven modules are:
   - live charts
   - hand-drawn charts and figures (the whiteboard sketch)
   - motion graphics
   - Cosmos cartoons (a narrated vignette for a backstory or a sidebar)
   - video insets
   - infographics
   - cinematic cut scenes

   **By default, use all seven at least once.** It shows what FLPT can do. Skip a module only when the brief or the user's notes say so, or when the talk is too short to fit all seven (then use as many as fit). Across the talk:
   - **A live chart:** real data, with its source.
   - **A hand-drawn chart or figure:** a concrete everyday metaphor for the most abstract idea.
   - **A motion-graphics build.**
   - **A Cosmos cartoon:** find the backstory worth telling (a person, a moment in history, an origin) and make it a vignette.
   - **A video inset:** a film or TV moment, or the user's own clip, that lands a point. The user supplies the clip.
   - **An infographic.**
   - **A cut scene:** a script line worth a short cinematic beat.
   - **At least one statement slide.**

## Write `bullets.md` exactly like this
```
# {{title}}: bullets

**Through line:** <one sentence>
**Arc:** <hook → … → ask, one line>

## Slide 1 — <title that says something (a claim or a sharp phrase), not a label>
- <bullet: one idea the presenter would say; 8–18 words>
- <2–5 bullets per slide>
Module: <one of the seven, and what it shows, e.g. "Live chart: bar, adoption by year, source X 2025">

## Slide 2 — …

## Sources
- <source title, publisher, date, URL> — supports slide N
```

Then finish with your report. Say which facts the fact-check should look at hardest.
