# Stage 2: Framework and diagrams

The user thinks in frameworks and brings rough notes, distinctions, anecdotes and hunches. The job is to find the through line, tighten the thinking, and give it a shape that holds up for 15–20 minutes. Use the user's own vocabulary, and credit prior art they may be reinventing. In the first run the user's knowing-that vs knowing-how split was Gilbert Ryle's, and the chess-vs-Go point was sharper framed as "judgment you can write down vs judgment that has to be learned."

## `Talk_Framework.md` structure

1. **Through line.** One sentence, plus a numbered logic chain of 6–8 steps: signal, why, what's protected, the purest case, the core insight, the bridge to the audience's business, why now, and what it means for them.
2. **Talk arc table.** Section, minutes, diagram. Aim for about 18 minutes in 8–9 sections.
3. **Section bullets.** Facts are verified and cited. Mark where the user tells their own war stories ("tell your story here"); those are the most persuasive minutes.
4. **Refinements and pressure points.** Where the framing needs tightening. Be candid, because this is where the user finds the most value.
5. **Lines worth stealing.** Short, quotable lines.
6. **Likely pushback,** with answers.

## Diagrams: `diagrams/talk-diagrams.html`

Write one local HTML page with 6–8 figures in inline SVG and HTML grids. They should draw the user's own distinctions: 2D matrices (complexity vs encodability), 2×2s, ladders, possible-world rings, cost curves, and comparison tables.

- **Colors:** use tokens with a dark-mode block.
- **Captions:** each figure gets a "Talking point" caption.
- **Check once:** screenshot it once, and fix any overlapping labels.

These sketches feed the deck. Keep labels short enough to survive at slide scale.
